1 · The three-mic plan — you already own the right mic for each job

These are three different jobs with three different best answers. Using one mic for all three is what makes a studio sound amateur.

JobMicPlugs intoWhy this one wins
Talking to Claude while you work Gaming headset Lenovo pink mic jack (+ green for the earcups) 2–3 cm from your mouth, so the garage loses and your voice wins. Hands stay on the work. Monitoring is wired, so there is no Bluetooth lag to fight.
Filming a repair Lapel / lavalier Pink jack, or the USB adapter Clipped to your shirt, the level stays constant as you lean over the bench and turn your head. This is what repair channels actually use.
Intro / voiceover / narration BM-800-class on a boom Pink jack via its XLR→3.5 mm kit cable, 5 V USB for bias (§13) Richest, fullest tone of the three — but only while you sit still in front of it.
Start with the headset. It is the only one of the three needing nothing at all — no adapter, no USB power, no boom, no meter reading. Plug it in and run hcc-mic-on on the Lenovo (installed 11-11; it forces the pink jack, unmutes, sets gain and loads the voice preset).

2 · Monitor tree — cables to pull

The map is §14's. It costs $0 and respects both measured GPU ceilings: Acer UHD 620 = 3 screens including its own panel; Lenovo Sandy Bridge = 2. 3 + 2 = exactly the five screens you asked for.

  • 1 × HDMI male–male — Acer → 32" onn Roku TV. This cable also carries your audio, so size it to reach the top of the tree.
  • 1 × VGA male–male, 15-pin, with thumbscrews — Acer → old Dell. The Dell is VGA-only and silent by design, so nothing is lost.
  • 1 × HDMI male–male — Lenovo → 24" Toshiba.
  • 3 × monitor power cords — one per external screen. Check whether the onn TV's cord is captive or detachable before you need it.
  • 1 × surge-protected power strip, 6+ outlets — you need 5 minimum: three screens plus two laptop chargers.
Two things only your eyes can settle (from §14, still open): (a) is there a blue 15-pin VGA socket on the Acer? (b) does the 24" Toshiba have an HDMI input? If the Toshiba is VGA-only it simply swaps places with the Dell and the map still works.

3 · Audio — your wired-speaker idea is the right call

Wiring the Bluetooth speaker is better than what §14 originally recommended. §14 measured the Bluetooth penalty: A2DP adds roughly 100–300 ms of latency — fine for music, ruinous for hearing yourself while recording, and it shows up as lip-sync drift on video — and Windows silently drops to narrowband the moment a call app grabs the speaker. A wire has neither problem.

  • 1 × USB → audio adapter — the one you have. Feeds the speaker by wire, and if it has a pink socket too it gives a far cleaner mic input than the Lenovo's onboard codec.
  • 1 × 3.5 mm male–male aux cable — adapter's green jack → the speaker's AUX IN.
  • 1 × 3.5 mm combo splitter (one TRRS plug → separate green + pink sockets) — only needed on the Acer, if it has a single combo headset jack rather than two. This is the adapter that makes a dual-plug headset work on a modern laptop.
  • 1 × USB-A extension — if the adapter won't reach the bench.
Check on the speaker: it needs an AUX IN jack for this to work. Most speakers of that class have one, but confirm — and note many only switch to AUX once a cable is physically inserted.

5 · Keyboard, mouse and network

One keyboard and mouse across all five screens is already built — Deskflow, configured and verified 11-10 (§12). It maps machines, not monitors, so adding screens adds no work.

  • 0 × KVM switch — do NOT buy one. Deskflow already does this over the network.
  • 2 × Cat5e/Cat6 patch cables — optional but recommended. Wired is steadier than Wi-Fi for Deskflow and for streaming.
  • 1 × keyboard + mouse at the bench (the Rapoo 2.4 GHz receiver is already on the Lenovo).
Layout gotcha (§14): put the GarageLaptop cell on the side the Lenovo physically sits on, or the mouse will cross the wrong screen edge.

6 · Power and cable management

  • 1 × surge-protected strip, 6+ outlets (counted in §2).
  • 2 × laptop chargers, left permanently at the bench — one per machine.
  • 1 × 5 V USB wall adapter — for the BM-800's bias power (§13), if you go that route.
  • Velcro straps or zip ties, split loom or raceway, and labels on both ends of every cable. With five screens behind a tree, one unlabeled HDMI costs you twenty minutes every single time.

7 · Adapters — the direction traps that waste an afternoon

AdapterVerdictWhy
Your VGA→HDMI cord Keep as a spare — not needed in the §14 map VGA is analog and HDMI is digital, so a passive cable cannot convert; it only works as an active, powered converter. And it is direction-specific — a VGA-source→HDMI-display box is a different unit from an HDMI-source→VGA-display box. The §14 map avoids conversion altogether by sending the Dell to a native VGA port.
3.5 mm combo splitter Probably needed — on the Acer Lets a dual-plug headset or lapel mic work on a laptop with only one combo TRRS jack.
USB DisplayLink video adapter Only for a 6th screen — and only on the Acer Both GPUs are at their ceiling at five screens. This corrects §14, which said "one machine": it has to be the Acer, because the Lenovo has no USB 3.
Audio interface / 48 V phantom supply Do not buy. Settled in §13. The capsule is a pre-charged electret wanting a few volts of bias, not 48 V phantom. The 09-17 note claiming otherwise was wrong and has been corrected.
