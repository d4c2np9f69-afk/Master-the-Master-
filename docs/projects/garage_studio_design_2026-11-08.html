Garage Repair Studio — Design v1

Drawn from Jeff's 5 photos (11-08) + the taped house plan (garage 13 × 24 ft). $0 — everything here is already in the garage.

1 · Floor plan (looking down, big door at the top)

Positions from the photos + plan-data.js. The east wall (house-door side) and the fridge / water-heater corner were not in the photos — drawn approximate, dashed.

The idea in one line

Split the garage in two with a canvas curtain just behind the man door. Front = yard & power equipment (dirt, fuel, noise). Back = a clean 13 × 11½ ft studio with three sets that all face into the room.

A · The Repair Set (filmed)

  • The red-cabinet bench on the back wall. It already looks like a real shop on camera — red doors, logos, pegboard, LED strip.
  • Bench top holds only the job being filmed. Three spots: test gear left · the mat center · iron + fume blower right.

B · Mission Control

  • The corner bench (where the onn monitor + Lenovo are now). Computer, big screen, UPS, mic arm.
  • Sits between the other two sets: swivel the chair and you are at either one. Claude's screen is visible from A.

C · The Talk Set

  • The antique desk pulled 3 ft off the WKU wall; you sit between the desk and the mural, facing into the room.
  • Intro, outro, "here's what came in", the before/after reveal. The hand-painted mural is the channel's backdrop — nobody else has it.

The curtain

  • Canvas drop cloths (the ones on top of the fridge in photo 2) hung on a wire across the garage, just behind the man door and the opener.
  • Four jobs: hides the yard gear from every camera, cuts the echo, keeps yard dust off the bench, and gives the studio a "room".
  • Overlap at the house-door end so you walk through without opening anything.

Light (all owned — the test shoot decides if it's enough)

  • Over A: the existing under-cabinet LED strip + two work lights at the bench corners, aimed in at ~45° (★ on the plan). Two from the sides = no hand shadows.
  • Over C: the ceiling can + one work light from the open side.

Sound

  • Mic arm moves from B to reach your seat at A. Curtain + drop cloths kill most of the echo. Fans off while rolling.

Air

  • The microwave salvage blower at the right end of A pulls solder smoke away from your face and the camera.

4 · What moves out of the studio (from the photos)

  • Off bench A: grease gun, wrenches, vise, license plates, the battery charger on the cabinets → yard side.
  • Off bench B: the Delta grinder + the microwave transformer → yard side / salvage shelf.
  • Off desk C: tools, the green caddy, fluids; the ladder hanging over the mural → yard ceiling hooks.
  • The folding table with the cardboard top → folds away against the curtain (comes out as a teardown table when needed).
  • Fluids, bags and jugs at the man door → yard side.

5 · Order of work

  1. You look at this and change it. Nothing moves until the layout is yours.
  2. Clear the back half, one set at a time (A, then B, then C).
  3. Hang the curtain.
  4. Power: a mounted strip per bench.
  5. Set the cameras, 2-minute test shoot, I review it frame by frame.
  6. Build what the test shows is missing — from salvage.

Ideas drawn from working electronics-channel setups: overhead + face camera pairing (jj5.net/wiki/Studio — uses a Razer Kiyo as its bench cam; briandorey.com workshop recording setup), neutral surroundings help white balance, zone the garage first and keep camera and light paths clear of the door tracks (journeymanhq.com).
