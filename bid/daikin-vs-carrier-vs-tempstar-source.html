The warranty — and whether it survives buying it yourself

Read verbatim this session out of Daikin LIMITED WARRANTY, Part No. PWDPKGETYURG rev 04/2025, which names "Models: DP14GM, DP14UM, DP3GM, DP3UM" — so it is the right certificate for this unit.
THE HEADLINE, AND IT IS GOOD NEWS: an over-the-counter purchase does not void the warranty. The only installer-related exclusion in the entire certificate reads, word for word:

“Units that are ordered over the Internet, by telephone, or by other electronic means unless the unit is installed by a dealer adhering to all applicable federal, state, and local codes, policies, and licensing requirements.”

The trigger is the ORDER CHANNEL, not who holds the wrench. A walk-in purchase at a counter in Carthage is not internet, not telephone, and not "other electronic means" — so on the plain text of the certificate that exclusion never fires, and the unit carries full warranty no matter who sets it. There is no clause anywhere requiring a licensed or factory-authorized installer, and no "unauthorized reseller" clause.
This corrects our own record. ac_unit_and_ductwork_2026-08-31.md says: "GOODMAN'S WARRANTY HAS NO '(or certified)' ESCAPE HATCH … Choosing Goodman therefore KILLS the friend-labour path outright." That was over-stated. The Daikin certificate, verified directly, conditions nothing on the installer for an in-person purchase. Caveat, stated plainly: the research reports Goodman's certificate carries the identical sentence, but the document verified here was Daikin's, not Goodman's. The 08-31 note should be amended, not deleted.

The exclusion that still bites"Damage or repairs required as a consequence of faulty installation or application." On all three units. A bad set gets denied on cause, not on who installed it — which is the real risk in a self-install, not the paperwork.
One conflict inside Daikin's own paperwork, found this session. The spec sheet markets "Lifetime Heat Exchanger Warranty (good for as long as you own your home), and 12-year Parts Limited Warranty". The certificate says 20 years plus one free replacement out to 99. Those are not the same promise. The certificate is the controlling document, so treat it as 20 years + one replacement, not lifetime. Do not rely on the brochure wording.

What a self-install actually involves (A2L)

From Daikin's own installation manual IM-IOD-3033. Much of this applies to the Carrier and Tempstar too — R-454B is also an A2L refrigerant.
The good news: a straight swap needs no refrigerant work on the new unit. A packaged unit is a sealed, self-contained circuit and ships factory-charged. No brazing, no line set, no evacuation, no charging — so EPA 608 certification is not required to set the new box, because the circuit is never opened. But recovering the refrigerant out of the old Nordyne before it is hauled off does require EPA 608 and proper recovery gear. That part is a certified-tech job regardless of who installs the new one.
Three A2L requirements that are easy to miss — and one of them hits the Carrier and Tempstar bids too.
  • The unit must never be left unpowered. "UNIT MUST BE POWERED EXCEPT FOR SERVICE" — it carries a powered refrigerant leak-detection and mitigation system that runs the blower to purge. No seasonal breaker-off.
  • THE DUCT UV LIGHT. "AUXILIARY DEVICES WHICH MAY BE A POTENTIAL IGNITION SOURCE SHALL NOT BE INSTALLED IN THE DUCT WORK. EXAMPLES … ARE HOT SURFACES … AND ELECTRIC SWITCHING DEVICES," and "ONLY AUXILIARY DEVICES APPROVED BY THE APPLIANCE MANUFACTURER … SHALL BE INSTALLED IN CONNECTING DUCTWORK." The owner-supplied REKO R2000 UV unit is an electrical device in the duct, and both Peters and Goodlettsville have agreed to install it free. Because R-454B is A2L as well, this needs checking on whichever unit is bought — it is not a Daikin-only issue.
  • Charge is tied to airflow. "this charge is based on 325 CFM per ton and minimum ESP … When air quantity or ESP is different than above, the refrigerant charge must be adjusted." If the measured static lands outside the band, somebody has to touch the charge — and that is back into certified refrigerant work.

What is now ANSWERED by the 09-21 proposal, and what is still open
  1. Are you an authorized Daikin dealer, and can you sell DP3GM3006031 over the counter? Their retail site lists no HVAC equipment brands at all, so this is unconfirmed. Ask for the full model number on the receipt.
  2. Is the 11th character a 3? Confirm R-32, not the R-410A ...041 version.
  3. What brand is the compressor? This is the one thing no document answers. Ask them to read the compressor label on the actual unit, or pull the R-32 repair-parts manual from their Daikin distributor portal. On the previous R-410A generation the 2.5-ton was a Copeland ZP25K5 but the 2-ton in the same line was a Rechi — the brand is not uniform across the line, so it cannot be assumed.
  4. What is the written price, and do they install? If D.T. McCall sells it and sets it, every warranty question collapses — they are a licensed, bonded and insured Tennessee contractor doing HVAC since 1978.
Bottom line.
  • Stainless: yes, and standard — better than Goodman, whose current sheet does not state a heat-exchanger material at all. The deal breaker is met.
  • Copeland: CONFIRMED. D.T. McCall put it in writing on their 09-21 proposal. No Daikin document names a compressor brand, so this was only ever answerable by the dealer — and they answered it.
  • The pad is the real cost. 51 in against 47.5 in — the same finding logged against this engine family on 09-09, now confirmed from Daikin's own dimension table — plus both plenums re-made to 16×16.
  • The warranty survives the counter purchase. The single best piece of news here, and it corrects an over-stated note in our own file.
  • Slightly worse on the two things you actually feel: EER2 10.6 against 11.05, and 76 dB against 73.
  • The honest comparison is not $4,500 against $11,400. It is a box with no duct work, no plenums, no pad, no commissioning and no labour warranty, against two finished jobs. Whether that trade is worth it is a labour question, not a hardware question.
