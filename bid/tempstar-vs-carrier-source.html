Tempstar PGB430060K000B vs Carrier 48NL-B300603

The short answer: it is the same machine with a different sticker. Tempstar is ICP (International Comfort Products), a Carrier Global company. The two product-data books are the same 39-page document - same page order, same tables, same numbers on every rating row, same accessory part numbers - with "Carrier" swapped for "Tempstar" and "Puron Advance" swapped for "R-454B". Of 750 text lines in the Tempstar book, 596 appear word-for-word in the Carrier book; the rest are brand names and page headers. What actually differs is the WARRANTY and what each BIDDER wraps around it.

Bottom line.
  • Hardware: no reason to prefer one box over the other - same coils, same blower, same compressor, same stainless heat exchanger, same sound, same weight, same charge. The only physical difference is a wire coil guard vs louvered panels around the outdoor coil.
  • Factory warranty: the Tempstar is slightly better - it carries a 3-year one-time whole-unit replacement the 48NL does not have. Parts and heat exchanger terms are identical (register within 90 days or you lose half of it).
  • Money over 10 years: Goodlettsville $11,354 vs Peters $11,470 ($11,375 without the permit line) - about $20-$120 apart. The $1,510 sticker gap is really the labour warranty, not the unit.
  • So the decision is the contractor, not the brand: who pulls the permit, who records the static pressures, who fabricates the plenums, who installs the garage run as drawn, who has the licence, who backs the labour. That is exactly what the bid gates measure.

Rust verdict: every corrosion-relevant part - base, cabinet steel and paint, heat exchanger, both coils, fan, burners - is the same material and the same spec on both units. The only difference is the outdoor coil cover: Carrier ships louvers, Tempstar ships a wire guard with louvers as an add-on (CPLOUVER006A00). What actually decides how long this unit lasts outdoors is item 7 in the scope - closing the base and pad penetrations - and keeping the drain clear; the old unit's rust came from water and critters getting in under it, and both new units are built to stop exactly that.

Customer ratings - what exists, honestly

  • Nobody publishes a rating for these two exact models. Package gas/electric units are a small market; Consumer Reports rates split systems and furnaces by brand, not packaged units, and had nothing for either model.
  • Brand-level, small samples: Tempstar air conditioners average about 3.7 / 5 on furnaceprices.ca ("reliability, runs great, quiet, efficient" are the recurring words); highperformancehvac.com shows 63 Tempstar reviews at 2.5 / 5 with 20 "very satisfied" - a site where unhappy owners go to post. Carrier's own 48NL page carries mixed owner reviews: "replaced our 27-year-old unit, much more efficient, super quiet" against "never worked well the first month, needed parts while brand new" - which is an installer story, not a box story.
  • Same factory, same parts: Tempstar, Heil and Comfortmaker are ICP, a Carrier Global company - same plants, same components, same part numbers (Today's Homeowner, thermostating.com). Carrier's premium lines go to higher SEER; at this 13.4 SEER2 tier the two books are the same document.
  • What the ratings really measure: on packaged units almost every complaint traces to the install - refrigerant charge, airflow, drain, gas pressure - which is why the static-pressure-on-the-invoice gate matters more than the badge.
